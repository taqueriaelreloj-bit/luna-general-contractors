Distinguish emergency work from reconstruction

Temporary protection and stabilization serve a different purpose from permanent repair. Keep their invoices and scopes identifiable, then document the materials and trades needed to restore the affected areas.

Work in an occupied property

If the property remains in use, agree on protected routes, room access, material storage and the point at which each area can be used again. The work plan should identify who moves belongings and how the construction area is separated.

How will access and protection work while the property is occupied?

Include a room sequence, daily access route, protection responsibilities and a handoff before each area is returned to use. Confirm these details against the actual scope rather than assuming every phase can happen around normal occupancy.

What needs to be confirmed before insurance-related reconstruction begins?

Identify which temporary work remains in the final construction scope. The written scope should also identify the selected products, work boundaries, required trade coordination and approval steps for changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you act as a public adjuster?

No. Luna General Contractors is a construction company. We prepare construction estimates, review repair scopes and complete approved repairs, while coverage decisions and policy interpretation remain with the insurer and policyholder.

Can you work from an insurance company estimate?

Yes. We can review the approved construction scope, compare it with visible site conditions and organize the roofing, exterior or interior repairs included in the project.

What types of insurance-related repairs do you handle?

Projects may include hail and wind repairs, roofing, siding, fascia, drywall, ceilings, texture, painting, trim, flooring, cabinets and water-damage reconstruction after the property has been stabilized.

Can one contractor coordinate several approved trades?

Yes. When the approved scope involves multiple trades, we can coordinate the construction sequence so roofing, drywall, paint, trim, flooring and related repairs are completed under one project plan.
